2. ~6% OF OCCURRENCE POINTS FALL IN MORE THAN ONE L4 AREA
================================================================================
On a 40,000-point sample, 38,984 matched an L4 area and 2,448 (~6.3%) matched
TWO OR MORE. Most frequently: Ukraine 322, Yunnan 266, Colombia 245, Reunion
235, Great Britain 211, Sichuan 203.

These are shared borders in the TDWG level-4 boundaries, NOT an artefact of
the planar fallback - the same duplication occurs under s2, and is identical
with and without st_make_valid().

Impact: downstream distinct() credits the taxon to BOTH areas, so L4 richness
is slightly inflated along borders. Needs a deliberate decision if these counts
go into the paper: accept, assign nearest centroid, or keep only the smallest
containing area.


================================================================================
3. GRIN EVIDENCE AT L4 IS MUCH WEAKER THAN THE RECORD COUNT SUGGESTS
================================================================================
Only 71,991 of 216,563 GRIN records (~33%) are placed at a specific L4 area via
`state`. The other 67% are SMEARED across every L4 area in their country.

Two consequences that are easy to state wrongly:
  - L4 GRIN presence is largely inferred, not observed.
  - It inflates apparent cross-source agreement at L4, because a smeared record
    corroborates every L4 area in the country at once. This is also why the
    native level-3 threshold test recovers so little (issue 8).

5. FRANCE IS INFLATED BY ITS OVERSEAS DEPARTMENTS
================================================================================
France ranks 3rd globally (3,909 taxa), ahead of India and Brazil, because
French Guiana, Guadeloupe, Martinique, Reunion and Mayotte all fold into the
France polygon - tropical species from three continents counted as French.

Cartographically correct (Natural Earth has no polygon for any of them) but
misleading in a country ranking. If a ranked table appears in the paper, France
needs a footnote, or those records need excluding from the ranking while
remaining on the map.

The same applies in miniature to the United Kingdom (Gibraltar), Norway
(Svalbard) and New Zealand (Tokelau), but the counts there are trivial.


================================================================================
6. CONTINENT-LEVEL GRIN RECORDS CORROBORATE VERY COARSELY
================================================================================
Expanding GRIN's region codes 001/003/009/021/027 means one "Europe" record
vouches for 50 countries at once. The >=2-source rule still applies - a
continent record can never confirm on its own - but it CHANGES what the rule
certifies: a taxon that WCFP places in Kenya and GRIN records only as "Africa"
is now "confirmed by 2 sources" in Kenya, where before it was single-source and
excluded.

Europe bites hardest: 170 source rows x 50 countries = 8,500 records. If
European counts look high relative to comparable regions, this is why. Worth a
sentence in the methods.


================================================================================
7. THE TEMP-DIRECTORY FIX IS PER-LAUNCH AND DOES NOT PERSIST
================================================================================
A run on 2026-09-12 died one minute in when Windows Automatic Maintenance
(SilentCleanup) deleted R's session temp directory mid-run. This workflow is
unusually exposed: openxlsx creates a workbookTemp_* folder under tempdir() for
EVERY saveWorkbook call, so a 37-minute run makes a dozen-plus attempts - a
dozen chances to collide with a cleaner. C: is currently at ~34 GB free of 476,
which is why cleanup runs hard.

The fix is the $env:TMP/TEMP/TMPDIR line in the launch block at the top of this
file. IT APPLIES TO THAT LAUNCH ONLY. Running the script from RStudio or a
fresh terminal reverts to the C: temp directory and can fail the same way.

The durable fix would be a TMPDIR line in .Renviron. Deliberately NOT done: it
would affect every R session on the machine, and that is the user's call.

8. CLAIMS THE DATA DOES NOT SUPPORT
================================================================================
8a. THE LEVEL-3 THRESHOLD TEST IS NOT A SUBSTANTIVE IMPROVEMENT
    The >=2-source test is applied AFTER aggregating to level 3, rather than
    rolling up the level-4 result. That is the correct test, and it is kept.
    But it was predicted to matter, and it does not:

        (L3 area, taxon) pairs from rolling the L4 result up  : 274,598
        (L3 area, taxon) pairs from testing >=2 sources at L3 : 274,806
          recovered by applying the threshold at L3           :     208

    208 pairs, 0.08%. Area coverage is 360 of 368 either way. The shortcut
    would have produced a map visually indistinguishable from this one.
    Why: GRIN's smearing (issue 3) already manufactures most cross-L4
    agreements at L4, so the native test only recovers what the smear missed.

    The script prints these three numbers on every run, so the claim stays
    auditable. Do not describe the native test as a methodological advance.

8b. RED LIST COVERAGE IS THE HEADLINE, NOT A FOOTNOTE
    Across the 25,200 taxa confirmed by >=2 sources at level 3 (2026-09-18 run;
    the patch in issue 4 moves up to 2 taxa out of NE):

        Not Evaluated  14,530  (57.7%)     Data Deficient       359
        Least Concern   8,677              Critically Endang.   129
        Vulnerable        582              Extinct in the Wild    3
        Near Threatened   475              Extinct                2
        Endangered        443              -> Threatened total 1,159

    57.7% have never been assessed, and of those that have, 80% are Least
    Concern. Any statement of the form "N food plant taxa in region X are
    threatened" is a statement about the assessed minority only.

8c. ONLY METHOD 3 REPRODUCES PUBLISHED METHODOLOGY
    From the paper's archive: Method 3 (the WCVP/TDWG level 3 join) reproduces
    data_summaries.R section 8, using the same wcvp_names.csv /
    wcvp_distribution.csv snapshots the paper ships.

    Custom to this script, needing their own justification: Method 1 (raw
    occurrence points), Method 2 (GRIN distributions), the >=2-of-3 synthesis,
    and every map. All the geography decisions are on the custom side.

9. PRESENTATION ITEMS LEFT OPEN
================================================================================
9a. All maps share one <h4> heading and are distinguished only by subtitle.
    Requested deliberately; noted because the heading alone does not identify
    which file is open.

9b. IUCN_possibly_extinct (5 taxa) and IUCN_possibly_extinct_in_the_wild (3)
    are separate flags from the category code - a "possibly extinct" taxon is
    usually still categorised CR - so they currently sit inside the
    CR/Threatened layers with no way to distinguish them. Could be their own
    layer or a popup field if wanted.

9c. Red List map file sizes scale with layer count, because each leaflet layer
    group embeds its own copy of the level 3 geometry - the 9-layer categories
    map carries it nine times (173.9 MB). Inherent to the approach.
    Two size estimates made during development were both ~35% low; budget well
    above a naive per-layer figure.

9d. The 75.9 MB L4 map is slow to open from file://. Almost none of that is
    geometry - it is the popup payload (460,536 area/taxon rows as JSON, parsed
    before the page is interactive) and file:// applies no compression.
